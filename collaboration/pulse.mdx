Coding agents are accelerating how fast code gets written — [55% faster task completion](https://github.blog/news-insights/research/research-quantifying-github-copilots-impact-on-developer-productivity-and-happiness/) in controlled studies, [~20% steady-state time savings](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13282v1) in enterprise deployments. But without alignment on *what* to build, that speed compounds cost instead of reducing it:

- **Stability degrades.** The [2024 DORA State of DevOps Report](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/) found that a 25% increase in AI usage quickened code reviews but **decreased delivery stability by 7.2%**. More code, shipped faster, with more incidents. Their conclusion: "writing code isn't the bottleneck to deploying reliable applications."
- **Senior engineers absorb the cost.** A [2025 study of experienced developers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10165) measured a 39-44% gap between perceived and actual productivity. Developers *felt* 20% faster but were **19% slower in practice**. The reason: senior engineers spent their time reviewing and coordinating misaligned agent output instead of shipping their own work — reviewing 6.5% more code while their own output dropped 19%.
- **Coordination breaks silently.** A [longitudinal study tracking teams from 2023-2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10956v1) found that even as AI adoption increased, core teamwork problems persisted: **misaligned communication across roles, lack of shared visibility into project goals, and ambiguity around accountability**.

Each of these problems — rework from instability, wasted senior eng time on coordination, agents executing on misaligned intent — has a direct dollar cost. DORA reports that high-performing teams spend [22% less time on unplanned rework](https://dora.dev/concepts/wellbeing/). Agent compute is [metered per request](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/plans), and a single misaligned prompt can consume an [order of magnitude more tokens](https://cursor.com/blog/aug-2025-pricing-teams) than a well-scoped one. Multiply that across a team running agents in parallel on the wrong direction, and the waste scales with the speed.

**Alignment is the force multiplier.** When teams agree on intent before agents execute, every dollar spent on agent compute goes further, every senior engineer's review time is spent on substance instead of correction, and every PR ships code that matches what was actually approved. Pulse makes this visible.

Pulse gives engineering leaders a direct, queryable relationship between the intent your team aligned on and the code that shipped.

## What Pulse does

Pulse is a conversational interface for understanding what's happening across your engineering org. Ask questions in natural language:

- "Which approved designs have drifted from what actually landed?"
- "Show me review bottlenecks this sprint"
- "What needs my attention this morning?"

Every answer references real planspaces, real code changes, and real review decisions — grounded in the intent your team wrote and the PRs that implemented it. Pulse connects to your team's tools (Slack, Notion, Linear, GitHub) so answers draw from the full context, not just what lives in Scott.

## Why this matters now

As agent usage scales, three cost and quality dynamics become material for leaders:

**Agent spend is increasingly metered.** GitHub Copilot's [premium request model](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/plans) and Cursor's [usage-based pricing](https://cursor.com/blog/aug-2025-pricing-teams) both show that agentic requests are variable cost — a single complex prompt can consume an [order of magnitude more tokens](https://cursor.com/blog/aug-2025-pricing-teams) than a simple one. Poorly scoped intent means wasted compute.

**Quality risk scales with speed.** AI-generated code can amplify insecure patterns — one study found [~40% of AI-generated programs contained vulnerabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09293). Without clear intent upstream, agents produce more code that needs more review, more rework, and more remediation.

**Rework is the hidden cost.** DORA reports that high-performing teams spend [22% less time on unplanned work and rework](https://dora.dev/concepts/wellbeing/) than low performers. When intent is aligned before agents execute, the rework loops that are invisible to traditional metrics — but brutal in practice — shrink.

Pulse makes these dynamics visible and measurable because Scott owns both sides of the handoff: the intent that started the work and the CI that links it to shipped code.

## How it works

1. Engineers share plans and proposals in [planspaces](/planspaces/overview)
2. Plans are [reviewed and approved](/collaboration/reviews) by stakeholders
3. [Scott CI](/developer-tools/scott-ci) traces each PR back to the approved intent automatically

This creates a data layer that didn't exist before — every PR linked to the plan that drove it, every plan linked to the PRs that implemented it. Pulse queries this layer to answer your questions.

## Metrics

Pulse surfaces four metrics that are only measurable because intent and code are structurally linked. Each is grounded in operational data, not self-reported estimates.

| Metric | What it measures | Why it matters |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Intent coverage** | % of shipped PRs backed by an approved plan | The adoption signal. Are your teams aligning before delegating, or shipping without a plan? Low coverage correlates with higher rework. |
| **Design fidelity** | How closely shipped code matches approved intent | Measures execution accuracy. Low fidelity means the plan was ambiguous or the implementation drifted — both actionable. Enterprise studies show [code review is 15% faster](https://github.blog/news-insights/research/research-quantifying-github-copilots-impact-on-code-quality/) and [53% more likely to pass tests](https://github.blog/news-insights/research/does-github-copilot-improve-code-quality-heres-what-the-data-says/) when intent is clear. |
| **Rework rate** | How often a team revisits intent after implementation starts | The money metric. Directly maps to DORA's finding that high performers spend [22% less on unplanned rework](https://dora.dev/concepts/wellbeing/). Tracks whether intent review is actually reducing churn. |
| **Review cycle time** | Time from review request to first decision | Measures alignment speed. Slow cycles surface bottlenecks — Pulse shows who and where. |

## Attention alerts

Pulse surfaces items that need action without requiring you to dig through individual planspaces:

- **Stale reviews** — review requests open longer than your team's norm
- **Design drift** — approved plans where the shipped code diverged significantly
- **Blocked work** — planspaces waiting on external dependencies or unresolved comments
- **High fork activity** — designs with multiple competing branches being explored (may need a decision)

## What this replaces

Most engineering leaders piece together visibility from standups, Jira dashboards, PR metrics, and asking people in Slack. That picture is always incomplete and always stale — it measures code throughput but not whether the team is building the right thing.

Pulse replaces that with a single surface where you can ask a question and get an answer grounded in what your team actually planned and what actually shipped.
