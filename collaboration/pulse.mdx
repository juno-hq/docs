Scott AI gives engineering leaders something that hasn't existed before: a direct, queryable relationship between the intent your team aligned on and the code that shipped. Pulse is where that visibility lives.

## Pulse

Pulse is a conversational interface for understanding what's happening across your engineering org.

Ask questions in natural language:

- "Which approved designs have drifted from what actually landed?"
- "Show me review bottlenecks"
- "What needs my attention this morning?"

Pulse answers by referencing planspaces, connected sources like Slack, notion, linear etc, code changes, and real review decisions — not summaries or estimates. Every answer is grounded in the intent your team wrote and the PRs that implemented it.

## How it works

The link between intent and code is what makes Pulse possible. When your team uses Scott:

1. Engineers share coding agent plans & proposals in [planspaces](/planspaces/overview)
2. Plans & proposals are [reviewed and approved](/collaboration/reviews) by stakeholders
3. [Scott CI](/developer-tools/scott-ci) traces the PR back to the approved intent automatically

This relationship enables pull request reviews to move faster because high level intent is also checked at CI time. This also unlocks new metrics that traditional tools can't measure.

## Metrics

Because intent and code are structurally linked, Pulse surfaces four metrics that traditional tools can't measure. These are grounded in operational data — not self-reported estimates.

| Metric | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Intent coverage** | Percentage of shipped PRs backed by an approved plan. This is the adoption signal: are your teams aligning on intent before delegating to agents, or shipping without a plan? |
| **Design fidelity** | How closely the shipped code matches the approved intent. Low fidelity means either the plan was ambiguous or the implementation drifted — both are actionable. |
| **Rework rate** | How often a team revisits the intent after implementation starts. Research shows high-performing teams spend [22% less time on unplanned rework](https://dora.dev/concepts/wellbeing/). This metric tells you if intent review is actually reducing churn. |
| **Review cycle time** | Time from review request to first decision. Measures how fast your team reaches alignment. Slow cycles indicate bottlenecks — Pulse surfaces who and where. |
